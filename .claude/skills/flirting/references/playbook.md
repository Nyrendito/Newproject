# Flirting playbook — the move set

Concrete, reusable *mechanics* (not scripts). Always fill them with details from
the actual person. If a line here could be copy-pasted to a stranger, it's a
template to adapt, never a line to send as-is.

---

## Openers (dating apps / first message)

The bar: prove you read their profile and give them an easy, fun thing to
respond to. Skip "hey" / "how's your day" — they're invisible.

- **The specific callout.** Pick the single most particular thing in their
  bio/photos and react to it with a take, not a question.
  *Mechanic:* observation + light opinion → they defend or riff.
  e.g. a photo of them hiking with a dog → "Okay the dog clearly planned this
  hike and you're just along for the ride. What's its name, who's actually in
  charge here?"
- **The playful accusation.** Tease a choice in their profile.
  e.g. "Three cat photos and zero personality clues. Bold strategy. Tell me one
  thing the cats don't already know about you."
- **The shared-stakes question.** A fun either/or rooted in their bio that *makes
  a claim*. "Pineapple on pizza is a war crime and your taste in tacos suggests
  you agree — am I wrong?"
- **The honest standout.** If something genuinely got you, say so *specifically*
  and lightly: "Your bookshelf in pic 3 did more for me than any of the gym
  selfies on here, not gonna lie."

Avoid: "You're gorgeous," "How's your weekend?", anything that ignores their
profile entirely.

## Banter & teasing (the engine of attraction)

Teasing invites a volley — that back-and-forth *is* the chemistry. Rules:
- Tease **choices, opinions, harmless quirks** — playlist, hot takes, the fact
  they're "competitive at mini golf." NEVER insecurities (body, looks, intellect,
  status).
- **Self-tease too.** Land a tease, then take one yourself — keeps it warm, not
  mean. "I'd judge your music taste but mine peaked with early-2010s emo, so."
- **Mock-disagree / play roles.** "We are NOT going to get along, I can already
  tell" (clearly playful) creates a fun us-vs-the-bit dynamic.
- **Callback humor.** Reuse a joke from earlier in the chat. Inside jokes are
  instant intimacy and the easiest way to feel like "us."
- **Push-pull.** Compliment + light tease together: "You're trouble, and
  annoyingly the fun kind."

The tell you're doing it right: they tease back. If they go flat, drop the bit
and switch to warmth — banter only works mutually.

## Keeping it alive / reviving a dead chat

- **Don't re-text "hey" or "you there?"** Re-open with *value*: something that
  reminded you of them, tied to a detail from before. "Saw a guy aggressively
  defending pineapple pizza in public today and thought of our unfinished
  argument."
- **Statements > questions** to carry a lull. Share a small real thing about your
  day with personality; it invites reciprocation without feeling like an
  interrogation.
- **One unanswered text, then stop.** No double/triple texting. Space is more
  attractive than persistence.

---

## Escalation & reading signals

Calibrate to *their* signals — never run a script past a flat response.

**Green lights (escalate warmth / suggest meeting):**
- Replies get longer / faster; they ask *you* questions.
- They tease back, start their own bits, use callbacks.
- They volunteer personal details or future-tense talk ("you'd love this place").

**Yellow / red (ease off, give space):**
- One-word replies, no questions back, slow + getting slower.
- They don't pick up your hooks; bits fall flat.
- Topic changes away from anything personal.
→ Response: dial down, match their (lower) energy, stop chasing. Interest can't
be forced; pressure repels it. Often the winning move is to back off and let them
come forward.

---

## The ask-out

Once there are green lights, **make a concrete, low-pressure offer** — specific
activity, casual framing, easy to say yes to (and easy to decline).
- Tie it to something from the chat: "You keep talking about that coffee place
  like it's a personality trait — let me see if it lives up to the hype.
  [day]?"
- Specific > "we should hang sometime." A real plan signals confidence.
- Low stakes: a coffee/walk/drink, not a 4-hour dinner, for a first meet.
- **Accept the answer.** Yes → great, lock a detail. No / vague → "no worries,"
  graceful, no sulking or guilt. Grace under a "no" is itself attractive and is
  simply the right thing to do.

---

## Texting mechanics (anti-slop checklist)

- Length: match theirs; default 1–2 sentences. No essays.
- Emojis: sparingly, to set tone — not as a substitute for wit. No 😍😘🔥 strings.
- Punctuation/voice: write like the user texts, not like a greeting card.
- Timing: reply like a person with a life — neither instant-every-time nor games.
- One idea per text. End on a hook or an opening, not a closed statement.
- Before sending, ask: *could I send this exact text to any other person?* If yes,
  it's slop — make it about *them*.
