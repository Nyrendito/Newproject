# Flirting (without the AI slop)

Most AI flirting is slop: recycled pickup lines, generic "hey gorgeous 😍"
openers, six-paragraph essays, and compliments so vague they'd fit anyone. Real
flirting is **specific, playful, light, and responsive** — it references *this*
person, leaves room for them to play back, and risks a little. The whole job of
this skill is to produce that, and to refuse the slop.

## Read this first — what makes flirting land

Flirting is not lines. It's a *dynamic*: two people building a private little
game. Grounded in the research (banter raises mutual attraction; teasing invites
back-and-forth; warmth + a little challenge beats pure praise), four levers do
almost all the work:

1. **Specificity** — react to something real and particular about them (their
   words, their photo, their bio, the moment), not a template. "You" beats "a
   girl/guy." Specific is the single biggest difference between charm and slop.
2. **Playful challenge (push-pull)** — light teasing, mock-disagreement, a
   raised eyebrow. It says "I'm not just trying to impress you," which is exactly
   what makes you attractive. Tease the *choices* (their playlist, their hot
   take), never their *insecurities* (looks, body, intelligence).
3. **Room to volley** — leave an opening they can hit back on. End on a hook, a
   question, a playful accusation — not a statement that closes the loop. Good
   flirting is a rally, not a serve.
4. **Calibrated risk + warmth** — show real interest (warmth) while keeping it
   light enough that either person can wave it off (low stakes). Confidence is
   being okay if it doesn't land.

## Hard rules — never produce slop

NEVER do these. They are the tells of AI flirting and they kill attraction:

- ❌ **Canned pickup lines / "rizz lines."** "Are you a magician?…", "Do you have
  a map?…" Anything that would work verbatim on a stranger. If it's copy-pasteable
  to anyone, delete it.
- ❌ **Generic compliments.** "You're so beautiful/perfect/amazing." Vague praise
  is forgettable and slightly creepy from a stranger. Compliment a *specific*
  thing, and prefer complimenting taste/choices/energy over appearance early on.
- ❌ **Emoji spam, pet names too early** ("hey beautiful 😍😘🔥"), and walls of text.
- ❌ **Over-eagerness** — double/triple texting, "I've been thinking about you all
  day" to someone you just matched, instant availability.
- ❌ **Interview mode** — "What do you do? Where are you from? Hobbies?" stacked
  questions with no flavor. Make statements and observations, not a questionnaire.
- ❌ **Negging / insults disguised as teasing**, anything sexual before there's
  clear mutual signal, and persistence after disinterest.
- ❌ **Trying too hard to be clever** — a forced pun is worse than a warm, real
  line. When in doubt, specific + warm > clever.

## Process

### Step 1 — Get the context you actually need
Don't write blind. In one short message, ask only for what's missing:
- **The medium & stage** — dating-app opener? reply to their message? texting a
  crush you know? in person? How far along are they?
- **The raw material** — paste their message / bio / the photo description / what
  was said. This is where specificity comes from; without it you're guessing and
  will produce slop.
- **The user's read** — what's the vibe so far, what do they want (a date? keep it
  alive? gauge interest?), and their natural voice (dry? goofy? sincere?).

If they gave enough already, skip the questions and go.

### Step 2 — Match their voice, not a "flirty persona"
Write in *the user's* voice — adapted to how they actually talk. The goal is the
best version of them, not a smooth-operator caricature. Keep it the length a real
person would text (usually 1–2 sentences). Read `references/playbook.md` for
the move set (openers that reference the bio, teasing templates, reviving a dead
chat, the ask-out, reading and matching signals).

### Step 3 — Give 2–3 options at different temperatures
Offer a small menu, not one take, labeled by vibe so they can pick what fits:
- a **warm/sincere** version,
- a **playful/teasing** version,
- (optional) a **bolder** version that risks a bit more.

Each must be specific to *their* situation. One line each. No preamble inside the
options — write them exactly as the user could send them.

### Step 4 — Say why, briefly
After the options, 1–2 lines on the *mechanic* — what each one is doing (e.g.
"the second one teases her playlist so she has something to defend → easy
volley"). This teaches the user to do it themselves next time, which is the real
deliverable.

### Step 5 — Read signals & calibrate
Help them read the response, not just send. If interest is dropping (short
replies, no questions back, slow), advise dialing *down* and giving space, not
chasing harder. If it's climbing (longer replies, teasing back, their own
questions), that's the green light to escalate warmth or suggest meeting. When in
real doubt about interest, the move is to make a low-pressure offer and accept the
answer gracefully. See `references/playbook.md`.

## The non-negotiable: respect and consent
Flirting is play *between willing people*. Enthusiasm is the point; pressure is
the opposite of it. A "no," a topic change, a dry reply, or going quiet means
ease off — gracefully, no guilt-trips, no "come on." Never help deceive,
manipulate, wear someone down, or pursue anyone who isn't clearly into it. Charm
that ignores the other person isn't charm. If a request crosses into harassment,
coercion, or deception, decline and say why.
