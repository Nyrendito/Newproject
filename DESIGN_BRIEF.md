# Design Brief — AI Glowup Skincare App

*For the designer. You're seeing this cold, so this explains the idea, who it's for, and what each screen needs to accomplish. It deliberately stops short of telling you how it should look — the visual design is yours. Think of this as the "what and why," not the "where to put the pixels."*

---

## 1. The idea in one line

You take a photo of your face, the app tells you what's going on with your skin and gives it a score, then hands you a routine and the exact products to glow up — and you re-scan over the following weeks to watch your score climb.

## 2. What it actually does

Three steps, dead simple:

1. **Scan** — user takes a selfie.
2. **Diagnose** — AI identifies their skin concerns (dehydration, texture, redness, dark spots, etc. — cosmetic, *not* medical) and gives an overall **skin score out of 100**.
3. **Fix** — it recommends a specific daily routine + the products to use, with reminders. Re-scanning later shows the score moving.

It is **not** a "rate how hot you are" app. It scores **skin condition** — the stuff you can actually improve — and the whole emotional promise is *progress*, not judgment.

## 3. Who it's for, and how it should feel

Gen-Z and young-millennial, **all genders** (this is not a pink "self-care" app, and it's not a toxic "looksmaxxing" app — it sits deliberately between those). People into glowups, skincare routines, self-improvement.

The feeling we're going for: **a clean, modern, slightly premium "personal skin coach."** Trustworthy and motivating, like a health app you'd actually believe — not a beauty filter, not a clinical dermatology tool, not a hype-y dark-pattern subscription trap.

## 4. The one principle that makes it different

**We score what the user can change, and we always frame it as a journey up.** A low score is never an insult — it's a starting line with a clear path. Every screen should feel like it's on the user's side. If a screen could make someone feel bad about their face with no way forward, it's wrong.

## 5. The flow (this is the spine)

Each screen below has a **job** and an **emotional beat**. Design them however reads best — these are the intentions, not layouts.

**A. Open / framing (1–2 quick taps)**
Job: set the goal and get them moving fast. Ask their #1 skin goal and rough age. Beat: "this is about *me* and where I want to get to." Keep it almost frictionless — the magic moment is the scan, so don't bury it behind a long quiz.

**B. The scan**
Job: capture a usable, consistent photo. Beat: a little anticipation. Practical reality you should design around: **lighting wrecks skin photos**, so guide them to good light and a consistent framing (the result is only as trustworthy as the input). This guidance needs to feel effortless, not like a hassle.

**C. "Analyzing…" moment**
Job: build perceived value while the AI works. Beat: "something serious is happening to *my* photo." A few seconds of visible processing makes the result feel earned.

**D. The result — THE hero screen (free)**
Job: deliver the score + the named concerns, **shown against their own photo**, so it feels specific and real (specificity is what makes people believe it — "redness around *your* nose," not a generic verdict). Beat: "wow, it actually sees me." This screen is also the thing people will screenshot and share, so it should look great *standing alone*. This is both the trust moment and the marketing.

**E. Personalize ("let's build your routine")**
Job: a short set of questions *now that they've seen value* — skin type, sensitivities, preferences. Beat: investment, anticipation. Same questions that would've felt like a chore up front now feel like building *their* plan.

**F. The paywall — gate the FIX, not the diagnosis**
Job: convert at peak desire. They've seen what's wrong; the fix (routine + exact products + progress tracking) is right there, locked. Beat: "I want the solution and it's one tap away." Show the value clearly, be honest about price (this is critical — see rules below).

**G. The plan (paid)**
Job: deliver the daily routine, the products, and reminders. Beat: "okay, I know exactly what to do." Should feel like a clear, doable care plan, not a wall of text.

**H. Re-scan / progress (paid, the reason they come back)**
Job: show the same face over time, score climbing, before/after side-by-side. Beat: "it's working, keep going." This is the retention engine — it has to feel rewarding. Important: a re-scan **only updates the score**; it does *not* hand them a new set of products. They're tracking progress on the routine they already have, not getting re-diagnosed every time.

## 6. The golden rule of the funnel

**Free = the reveal (score + concerns). Paid = the fix (routine + products + progress).** We give away the diagnosis because it builds trust and it's shareable; we charge for the solution and the journey. Don't gate the reveal — gate what comes after it.

## 7. Tone & reference apps

Apps worth pulling a feeling from (genre and polish, not literal copying):

- **Cal AI** — the onboarding → scan → result → paywall *rhythm*. Good model for pacing.
- **Oura / Gentler Streak / Finch** — how to show a score and progress in a way that feels kind and motivating, not judgmental.
- **PictureThis / Plantum** — "photo → here's what it is → here's the care plan" done warmly.
- **Headspace / Calm** — the level of calm, premium polish and restraint we want.
- **BetterMe / Noom** — the before/after *transformation* arc.

Avoid the **Umax / looksmaxxing** aesthetic — the harsh "you're a 6/10" energy. We want their engaging score mechanic *without* the cruelty.

## 8. Hard rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Non-toxic.** Score skin, never attractiveness. Always pair a problem with a path forward.
2. **Trustworthy, not sleazy.** Honest pricing — lead with the real annual price, don't hide the math. A paywall people feel tricked by gets screenshotted and mocked, which kills us since we grow through sharing.
3. **Specific = believable.** The result should feel tailored to *their* face, not generic.
4. **Simple.** This does one thing well. Resist adding features — no social feed, no store, no journal. Scan → fix → track. That's it.

## 9. What's explicitly yours

You're the designer — the visual system is your call: the look, the color, type, the score visualization, how the result reads, the motion, the overall feel. The brief above is *what each screen must accomplish and the emotion it should carry*. Make it beautiful and make it feel like something people trust with their face.

The single most important screen to nail is **D, the result/reveal** — it's where trust is won and where our sharing/growth comes from. If that screen is great, the app is great.
